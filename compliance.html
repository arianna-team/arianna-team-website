Cyber Resilience Act · Regulation (EU) 2024/2847

What the CRA requires, and how ARIANNA does it

The CRA sets cybersecurity requirements for products with digital elements sold in the EU. Annex I, Part II defines how manufacturers must handle vulnerabilities, and Article 14 sets the reporting obligations. Fines reach €15M or 2.5% of global turnover, and non-compliant products lose CE marking.

01

Know every component

Identify and document components, including an SBOM.

ARIANNA's SCA tools build an exact SBOM for every firmware version, and the hardware catalogue builds the HBOM. No source code or binaries are shared.

02

Handle vulnerabilities for the support period

At least five years, unless the product is expected to be used for less.

Every component of every version in the field is checked daily against NIST NVD, CVE.org, GitHub Security Advisories and ENISA's EUVD.

03

Fix without delay

Address and remediate vulnerabilities, including through security updates.

The pre-triage engine ranks findings by risk and exploitability, and remediation deadlines follow your policy, with reminders before they expire.

04

Report exploited vulnerabilities

Early warning within 24 hours, notification within 72 hours, final report within 14 days of a fix.

Vulnerabilities already known to be exploited, including those reported to ENISA by others, are flagged and prioritised automatically. When you detect exploitation in your own product, record it in ARIANNA: the Device Model shows at once which products and versions are affected.

05

Keep the evidence

Technical documentation, kept for market surveillance authorities.

Every triage decision and status change is recorded. Export SBOMs and vulnerability status as CycloneDX, SPDX, VEX or PDF whenever you're asked.

Harmonised standards for the CRA are being developed as the EN 40000-1-x series.

Radio Equipment Directive

RED cybersecurity, in force since August 2025

The RED Delegated Act (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30, amended by (EU) 2023/2444) adds cybersecurity requirements for in-scope radio equipment placed on the EU market, from Wi-Fi and Bluetooth devices to anything that connects to the internet.

  • Vulnerability assessment evidence. Show that known vulnerabilities in your radio products' components have been identified and assessed.
  • Risk management. Document applicability, accepted risks and mitigations, backed by security claims.
  • A head start on the CRA. The same Device Model and process carry over when the CRA fully applies.

IEC 62443

The bar in industrial markets

Industrial and energy customers increasingly require suppliers to follow IEC 62443. Its secure development lifecycle (62443-4-1) expects defined processes for handling security issues and managing security updates.

  • A documented process. ARIANNA's four-stage pipeline gives every vulnerability a consistent, auditable path.
  • Policy-based deadlines. Remediation timeframes by risk level, with expiring and overdue items highlighted.
  • Evidence for assessors. Full action history and exports for audits and customer reviews.

Medical devices
